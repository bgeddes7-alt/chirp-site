Kids art guide

How do I keep my kid's school artwork without keeping all the paper?

Photograph each piece and file the photo under that kid. The paper can go after that. Keep one small box of originals per kid each year for the pieces you can't part with, and print the photos as a book when the school year ends.

By Brian Geddes, dad of three · Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

Friday, 3:15. The backpack lands on the kitchen floor and a stack of paper slides out. A turkey made from a hand. A worksheet with a sticker on it. Two copies of the same snowman. Something purple my daughter swears is a horse. It goes on the counter with last week's stack, which is on top of the stack from the week before.

You can't keep all of it. You also feel like a monster putting a horse in the recycling. Here is what finally worked in our house.

Decide what stays as paper

Pick one container per kid per school year. A shallow bin or a big envelope is plenty. That box is the limit, so when it's full, something has to come out before something goes in.

What earns a spot in the box:

  • Anything with their handwriting on it, especially their name spelled wrong.
  • Handprints and footprints. Those get you later.
  • The piece they made a big deal about at pickup.
  • One self portrait a year, so you can watch the stick figure grow arms.

Everything else gets photographed and can go.

Photograph it so it looks good

Bad photos are why a lot of parents give up and keep the paper. A few habits fix most of it.

  • Use daylight near a window and skip the flash. Flash bounces off crayon and marker.
  • Lay the art flat on a plain surface. A dark table makes the edges easy to see.
  • Hold the phone straight above the page so it doesn't come out shaped like a trapezoid.
  • For a giant painting, stand on a chair. Tell nobody.

For clay, macaroni and anything with glitter falling off it, take a photo from the front and one from the side. Then make your peace.

File it so you can find it again

A camera roll with hundreds of drawings mixed in with screenshots is a digital pile. Same problem, smaller counter. Give each kid their own album, and put the month or the school year in the name. When grandma asks for "the one with the dog," you will find it before she hangs up.

Do the photos in batches. Friday afternoon when the backpack comes home is a good habit to attach it to. A few minutes and the counter is clear.

Let your kid in on it

Kids handle this better when they get a vote. Ask them which one or two from the week go in the box. Snap a photo of them holding a favorite now and then. Those end up being the photos you love most.

Then put the rest in the recycling when they're asleep. Bottom of the bin. Under the cereal box. I'm not proud of it, but I have three kids and one counter.

Turn the year into a book

This is the part that makes letting go of the paper feel fine. At the end of the school year, gather that kid's photos into one book, one drawing per page, with the month on each one. Costco, Walgreens and Shutterfly all print photo books. A book takes up a few inches on a shelf, and nobody has to keep a bin in the garage until the kid moves out.

Grandparents love these. Send them the file and let them print their own copy.

Questions

Is it okay to throw away my kid's artwork?

Yes, once you've saved a good photo of it. Most families keep a small box of originals and let the rest go. A printed book at the end of the year gives you something to hold.

How much of my kid's art should I keep?

Pick a container size and let it set the limit. One small box per kid per school year keeps it out of the garage.

What's the best way to scan kids' artwork with a phone?

Lay it flat in daylight, skip the flash, and hold the phone straight above the page. A scanning app that flattens and crops the page saves you from editing each photo.

What do I do with 3D art and big paintings?

Photograph 3D pieces from the front and the side. For a big painting, stand above it on a chair. Keep one favorite and photograph the rest.

Can I turn my kid's artwork into a book?

Yes. Costco, Walgreens and Shutterfly print photo books. Plume makes a print-ready PDF of each school year with a chapter for each kid.
