How do I share my kid's artwork with grandparents who live far away?

Photograph each piece and put the photos in one shared album the grandparents can open on their phone. Add a line from your kid about what each one is. Every so often, mail a real original, and at the end of the school year send them the whole year as a book.

By Brian Geddes, dad of three · Updated November 2026 · 5 min read

Sunday night video call. Grandma asks what everybody made at school this week. A five year old holds a drawing about two inches from the camera, so all she sees is a blur of green. Then he drops it and wanders off to find a snack. She says it's beautiful. She has no idea what it was.

Grandparents who live far away want to see this stuff. You want them to see it without texting forty blurry photos at 10pm. Here's a setup that takes a few minutes a week.

Pick one shared album

Group texts bury art under memes and dinner plans. Give the art its own home that the grandparents can open whenever they want.

If they have an iPhone or iPad, a Shared Album in the Photos app works well. You invite them, they get a notice when you add something, and they can leave a comment under each picture. Make one album per kid so Grandpa isn't guessing whose dinosaur that is.

If they use a different kind of phone, any photo service with a share link does the job. The part that matters is that it stays in one spot.

Send a few at a time, with the story

Grandparents light up over the story behind a drawing. Once they know it's the cat fighting a volcano, you'll get a phone call about it.

  • Pick the best few from the week. Grandparents don't need every worksheet.
  • Ask your kid what it is and type their answer as the caption, word for word.
  • Now and then, record a short video of your kid holding it up and explaining. Ten seconds is plenty.
  • Do it at the same time each week. Friday after the backpack comes home works for us.

The captions end up being the best part. Grandma will quote "the cat is winning" back to you for years.

Mail the real thing sometimes

Grandparents like having something to stick on their own fridge. Once a month or so, let your kid pick one piece to mail. They can write a note on the back and put the stamp on, which for a five year old is the highlight of the week.

Take a good photo of it before it goes in the envelope. Lay it flat by a window, skip the flash, and hold the phone straight above it. Then you keep a copy, and the grandparents get the original with the glitter still falling off.

Make the video call easier

If your family does regular calls, open the shared album before you dial so everybody is looking at the same picture. The kid can talk about it while the grandparents see a clear photo on their own screen. No more holding art up to the camera and hoping.

Send the year as a book

At the end of the school year, put that year's art into one book, one drawing per page, with the month on each. Costco, Walgreens and Shutterfly print photo books, and you can ship a copy straight to the grandparents' house. If you have the book as a file, send them the file and let them print their own.

Questions

What's the easiest way to send kids' art to grandparents?

Photograph each piece and add it to one shared photo album they can open on their phone or tablet. On iPhone, a Shared Album in Photos lets you invite them and they can comment on each picture.

Should I mail the original artwork?

Mail one now and then. A real piece of paper on their fridge means a lot. Photograph it before it goes in the envelope so you keep a copy.

How often should I send artwork to grandparents?

Once a week or so is plenty. A small batch with a note about each piece gets more attention than forty photos on a Tuesday night.

What if the grandparents aren't good with technology?

Mail prints or a printed book. Costco, Walgreens and Shutterfly print photo books, and you can have a copy shipped straight to them.

Can I make a book of my kid's art for the grandparents?

Yes. Plume turns each school year into a print-ready PDF with a chapter for each kid. Send them the file or print a copy and mail it.
