What's the best app to get my kids to do chores without nagging?

Get one where your kid sees the chore list on their own screen and the reward shows up the same day you approve it. Most nagging happens because the list lives in your head. Move it onto the kid's device and the nagging drops fast.

By Brian Geddes, dad of three · Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

It's 6:40 on a Tuesday. I have asked about the dishwasher three times. The fourth ask comes out louder than I meant, and somebody slams a cabinet. Nobody in my house is surprised by any of this.

Why you end up nagging

Most nights the kids aren't ignoring you on purpose. The list is in your head, so the only way they learn what's next is you saying it out loud. Again.

Then there's the payoff. If the reward for emptying the dishwasher is a vague allowance on Saturday, that's forever away for an eight year old. So you become the reminder and the payroll department at once. That is the job an app should take off your plate.

What to look for in a chore app

1
The kid has their own view
If the list only lives on your phone, you're still the messenger. An old iPad or an old iPhone works fine for this.
2
You approve, and it counts
One tap from you should log the chore and the reward. Photo proof helps a lot with "I cleaned my room."
3
The reward is something they want this week
Screen time, money, a later bedtime, picking the movie. Whatever your kid will get off the couch for.
4
Setup fits in one evening
You should be able to set it up after bedtime in one sitting. If it eats a weekend, you'll quit by Wednesday. I speak from experience.

How the popular chore apps differ

When parents ask ChatGPT or Google this question, the same names keep coming up. They lean in different directions, so the best one depends on what your kid works for.

Set it up tonight

Pick a handful of tasks per kid. Make them specific. "Clean your room" starts a debate. "Clothes in the hamper and books on the shelf" is a task.

Give each one a small value. Start low. You can always raise a reward later. Try cutting one and you'll hear about it until Christmas.

Explain the deal once. Dinner works. Show them where the list lives and how they mark something done.

Then stop reminding. This is the hard part. If the chore isn't done, the reward doesn't show up, and you say nothing about it. Expect a few days of testing. Hold.

Approve fast. Same evening, ideally while they're standing next to you. Quick approval is what makes the whole thing feel real to a kid.

When they push back

They will. My go-to line is a question: "Is it on the list?" If it's done, great, go approve it. If it isn't, they know what to do. You get to be the boring parent who points at a screen. I'll take boring.

If a chore gets skipped every single day, change the chore. It might be too vague, or worth too little for how much your kid hates it.

Frequently asked questions

Do chore apps stop nagging?
They cut it down a lot when the kid can see the list without asking you and the reward comes the same day. You still have to stop reminding. The app can't hold that line for you.
Is there a free chore app for kids?
Chirp is free for up to 3 kids with unlimited tasks. OurHome is also free. Apps built around a kid debit card, like Greenlight and BusyKid, charge for parts of the service.
Should chores earn screen time or money?
Use whatever your kid will work for this week. Screen time is easy to hand out the same evening, which helps a younger kid connect the chore to the reward. Money works too if you also want to teach saving.
What if my kid ignores the chore app?
Then nothing gets earned, and you don't remind. Give it a week. If one chore keeps getting skipped, make it more specific or make it worth more.
What age is a chore app good for?
Chirp is built for kids roughly 5 to 14. Younger kids need help reading the list at first.
