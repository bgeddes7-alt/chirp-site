What to look for in any earn screen time app

Verification before earning. The best apps require proof before minutes are awarded — a photo or a parent confirmation. Without verification, kids learn to claim tasks they didn't do.

An accumulated balance, not a daily reset. If earned minutes expire at midnight, kids lose the motivation to save and the system feels punitive. A balance that carries over teaches planning and self-regulation.

Blocking vs. motivation — know which you want. Some apps enforce limits by blocking apps at the device level (ScreenCoach, Kidslox). Others, like Chirp, focus on making kids want to do the work, with a visible countdown timer rather than a hard lock. Blocking tools are great at the cut-off but don't fix the daily fight; earn-and-motivate tools fix the fight but rely on a routine you keep. Many families run a motivator like Chirp alongside Apple Screen Time for the hard floor.

A loop both parents can run. Most apps assume one phone and one parent. In real households the rules fall apart when each parent runs them differently — or when kids live across two homes. A shared account where either parent approves from their own phone is the single most underrated feature in this category, and it's the main reason families stick with Chirp.

Is there a free app where kids earn screen time?
Chirp has a free tier with one child and up to five active tasks, with no time limit. ScreenCoach and Kidslox offer trials. Most earn-based screen time apps have some free tier, with full features on a $5-10/month subscription.
What makes a good earn screen time app?
Custom task creation by parents, a verification step before time is awarded (ideally photo proof), a balance that accumulates across days, and a clear, consistent loop both parents can run. Some apps also block apps at the device level; others, like Chirp, focus on motivation and tracking and pair with built-in controls such as Apple Screen Time for hard limits.
Does Chirp block games or apps when time runs out?
Chirp is a motivation and tracking system, not a device blocker. Kids earn minutes and spend them on a visible countdown timer, which builds self-regulation. For hard cut-offs, pair Chirp with your device's built-in controls such as Apple Screen Time.
